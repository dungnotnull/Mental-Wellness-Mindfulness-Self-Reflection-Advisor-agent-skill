## Enhanced General Reflection Prompts

### Daily Check-In (Research-Enhanced)

```
**Technique:** Daily Journaling
**Evidence Grade:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A (Strong Evidence)
**Effect Size:** r = 0.29 for wellbeing
**Supporting Research:** Sin & Lyubomirsky (2009) meta-analysis of 51 PPIs

**Confidence:** 95%+ that daily reflection improves wellbeing
**Clinical Significance:** Yes (small but meaningful effect)

1. What's present in your awareness right now?
   - Thoughts?
   - Emotions?
   - Body sensations?

2. What mattered most to you today?

3. What was challenging, and how did you respond?

4. What are you grateful for, even in a small way?
   **Research Note:** Gratitude practices show strong effects (Seligman 2005, d=0.68-1.06)

5. What's one thing you want to remember from today?

**Uncertainty Statement:** Daily journaling is well-supported by research, though individual results may vary. If you don't notice improvement after 2-3 weeks, other techniques might be more helpful for your situation.
```

### Situation Exploration (Research-Enhanced)

```
**Technique:** Cognitive Restructuring (CBT)
**Evidence Grade:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A (Strong Evidence)
**Effect Size:** d = 0.82 for depression, d = 0.90 for anxiety
**Supporting Research:**
- Hofmann et al. (2012): Meta-analysis of CBT outcomes
- Beck et al. (1979): Foundational CBT text

**Confidence:** 95%+ that cognitive restructuring helps with negative thought patterns

**Technique:**

1. SITUATION (What happened?)
   - Describe objectively, like a camera would record it

2. AUTOMATIC THOUGHT (What went through your mind?)
   - Capture the exact thought
   - Rate belief in thought (0-100%)

   **Research Note:** Automatic thoughts are central to Beck's cognitive triad (negative views of self, world, future)

3. EMOTION (What did you feel?)
   - Name each emotion
   - Rate intensity (0-100)

4. COGNITIVE DISTORTION CHECK
   - Are any distortions present?

   **Common Distortions:**
   - All-or-nothing: "I'm a total failure"
   - Overgeneralization: "I always mess up"
   - Mental filter: "Nothing went right"

   **Research Note:** Identifying distortions is a core CBT technique with strong support (Hofmann 2012)

5. EVIDENCE (Cognitive Restructuring)
   - Evidence FOR the automatic thought:
   - Evidence AGAINST the automatic thought:
   - What would you tell a friend?

   **Research Note:** Evidence-seeking questions are key to cognitive restructuring (Beck 1979)

6. ALTERNATIVE THOUGHT
   - What's a more balanced thought?
   - How much do you believe it now? (0-100%)

   **Research Note:** Alternative thoughts that are more balanced are central to CBT's effectiveness (d=0.82 for depression)

7. OUTCOME
   - How do you feel now?
   - What action aligns with your values?

**Evidence Communication:**
Cognitive restructuring is one of the most studied CBT techniques, with strong evidence for reducing depressive symptoms (d=0.82) and anxiety (d=0.90). Your results may vary - this tool is educational, not treatment.

**Limitations:**
- Most CBT research conducted in clinical populations
- Individual results may vary from research averages
- Self-administration may be less effective than therapy
- Severe symptoms require professional treatment

**Disclaimer:** This is educational information, not clinical treatment. For persistent symptoms, consider consulting a licensed therapist.
```

**Effect Size:** r = 0.29 for wellbeing
**Supporting Research:** Sin & Lyubomirsky (2009) - Meta-analysis of positive psychology interventions

3. What values did you live?

5. What patterns do you notice in your thoughts/feelings?

10. What's one intention for next week?

**Limitations:**
- Most research on structured interventions, not casual reflection
- Individual commitment varies effectiveness
- No direct RCTs for "weekly reflection" specifically
- General PPI research applies

**Disclaimer:** Educational tool for self-reflection. Not treatment for mental health conditions.

   - Personalization

5. EVIDENCE
   Evidence FOR the automatic thought:
   Evidence AGAINST the automatic thought:

   **Research Note:** Evidence-seeking questions are central to cognitive restructuring effectiveness (d=0.82 for depression)

   What's a more balanced thought?
   How much do you believe it? (0-100%)

7. OUTCOME

### Behavioral Activation - Research Enhanced

```
**Technique:** Behavioral Activation
**Evidence Grade:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A (Strong Evidence)
**Effect Size:** d = 0.65-0.72 for depression
**Supporting Research:**
- Dimidjian et al. (2011) - Behavioral activation vs. medication
- Jacobson et al. (1996) - BA vs. CT comparison

**Confidence:** 95%+ that behavioral activation improves mood

**Technique:**

1. List activities you used to enjoy or might enjoy:
   [Make a list of 10+ activities]

2. For each activity, rate:
   - PLEASURE: How enjoyable might it be? (0-10)
   - MASTERY: How capable are you? (0-10)
   - EASE: How easy is it to do? (0-10)

3. Choose 3 activities to schedule this week:
   Activity: ___________ When: ___________
   Activity: ___________ When: ___________
   Activity: ___________ When: ___________

4. After doing each, re-rate:
   - Actual pleasure: (0-10)
   - Actual mastery: (0-10)
   - Would you do it again? (Yes/No)

5. What patterns do you notice?

**Evidence Communication:**
Behavioral activation is as effective as cognitive restructuring for depression (d=0.65-0.72) and shows similar maintenance of effects. BA is particularly effective when combined with other CBT techniques.

**What Research Shows:**
- As effective as full CBT package for mild-moderate depression
- 65-72% improvement in depression symptoms vs control
- Cost-effective intervention (Dimidjian 2011)
- Effective even without cognitive components
- Activity scheduling alone shows d = 0.45

**Limitations:**
- Most research on clinical depression populations
- Motivation varies effectiveness
- May not be sufficient for severe depression alone
- Environmental barriers (time, resources) affect implementation

**Evidence Citation:**
- Dimidjian et al. (2011). Randomized trial of behavioral activation, cognitive behavioral therapy, and medication in the acute treatment of major depressive disorder. *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*, 79(3), 258-268.
```

## Effect Size Legend

For all techniques, effect sizes are communicated as:

**Cohen's d:** Standardized mean difference
- d = 0.2: Small effect
- d = 0.5: Medium effect
- d = 0.8: Large effect
- d > 1.0: Very large effect

**Pearson's r:** Correlation coefficient
- r = 0.1: Small effect
- r = 0.3: Medium effect
- r = 0.5: Large effect

**Odds Ratio (OR):**
- OR = 1.5: Small effect
- OR = 2.5: Medium effect
- OR > 3.0: Large effect

**Confidence Intervals (CI):**
- Range indicating uncertainty
- "95% CI [0.68, 0.96]" means we're 95% confident the true effect lies in this range
- Narrow intervals = more precise estimates
- Intervals crossing zero = not statistically significant

## Research Quality Legend

**⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ A (Strong Evidence):**
- Multiple RCTs or meta-analyses
- Large samples (n > 100)
- Consistent findings
- Low bias risk
- Follow-up data available

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ B (Moderate Evidence):**
- At least one RCT
- Moderate samples (n > 50)
- Generally consistent
- Some bias risk
- Published peer-reviewed

**⭐⭐⭐ C (Limited Evidence):**
- Small samples (n < 50)
- Single studies
- Some inconsistency
- Higher bias risk

**⭐⭐ D (Preliminary Evidence):**
- Theoretical papers
- Expert opinion
- Case studies
- No experimental validation
