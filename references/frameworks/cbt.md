# CBT Framework — Cognitive Behavioral Therapy Principles

**Purpose:** Operationalize CBT principles into concrete techniques for journaling, reflection, and cognitive restructuring. These are educational tools, not clinical interventions.

> **Source:** Based on Beck (1979) Cognitive Therapy and the Emotional Disorders; Burns (1980) Feeling Good; and subsequent CBT research.

## Core CBT Model

### The Cognitive Triangle

```
        Thoughts
       ↙        ↖
      ↵          ↖
    Emotions ←→ Behaviors
```

**Key Insight:** Our thoughts influence our emotions and behaviors. By changing how we think about situations, we can change how we feel and act.

### Situation → Thought → Emotion → Behavior

| Situation | Automatic Thought | Emotion | Behavior |
|-----------|-------------------|---------|----------|
| Friend doesn't text back | "They don't care about me" | Sad, rejected | Don't reach out, withdraw |
| Friend doesn't text back | "They're probably busy" | Neutral | Send casual follow-up text |
| Friend doesn't text back | "I must have done something wrong" | Anxious, guilty | Over-apologize when they do reply |

**Principle:** Same situation, different thoughts → different emotions and behaviors

## CBT Techniques for Journaling

### 1. Thought Records

**Purpose:** Identify automatic thoughts and examine their accuracy

**Structure:**
```
1. SITUATION (What happened?)
   - Describe objectively, like a camera would record it

2. AUTOMATIC THOUGHT (What went through your mind?)
   - Capture the exact thought
   - Rate belief in thought (0-100%)

3. EMOTION(S) (What did you feel?)
   - Name the emotion(s)
   - Rate intensity (0-100)

4. ALTERNATIVE THOUGHT (What's another way to see this?)
   - What evidence supports the automatic thought?
   - What evidence contradicts it?
   - What would you tell a friend in this situation?
   - What's a more balanced thought?

5. OUTCOME (How do you feel now?)
   - Rate belief in alternative thought (0-100%)
   - Rate emotion intensity now (0-100)
```

**Example:**
```
SITUATION: Submitted report at 5pm, boss didn't acknowledge it
AUTOMATIC THOUGHT: "My work is terrible, they're ignoring it because it's bad" (Belief: 85%)
EMOTIONS: Anxious (80%), Sad (70%)

EVIDENCE FOR:
- I rushed some parts
- I'm not confident about the analysis

EVIDENCE AGAINST:
- Boss is usually responsive
- I've gotten good feedback before
- No one has said my work is bad

ALTERNATIVE THOUGHT: "The boss might be busy or left early. I've done good work before." (Belief: 70%)

OUTCOME: Anxious (50%), Sad (30%)
```

### 2. Identifying Cognitive Distortions

**Common Distortions:**

| Distortion | Description | Example |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| **All-or-Nothing** | Seeing things in black/white categories | "If I'm not perfect, I'm a failure" |
| **Overgeneralization** | Seeing a pattern based on one event | "I always mess things up" |
| **Mental Filter** | Focusing only on negatives | "Nothing went well today" (ignoring positives) |
| **Disqualifying Positive** | Rejecting positive experiences | "They only complimented me to be nice" |
| **Jumping to Conclusions** | Mind reading or fortune telling | "She didn't look at me, so she must be mad" |
| **Magnification** | Blowing things out of proportion | "This is a disaster" |
| **Emotional Reasoning** | Assuming feelings reflect facts | "I feel guilty, so I must have done something wrong" |
| **"Should" Statements** | Rigid rules about self/others | "I should never make mistakes" |
| **Labeling** | Overgeneralized labels for self/others | "I'm a loser" |
| **Personalization** | Assuming responsibility for things outside control | "The party failed because of me" |

**Journaling Prompt:**
"Look at your automatic thought from above. Are any of these distortions present? Which one(s)?"

### 3. Behavioral Experiments

**Purpose:** Test beliefs through action

**Structure:**
```
1. PREDICTION (What do you think will happen?)
2. EXPERIMENT (What will you do?)
3. OUTCOME (What actually happened?)
4. CONCLUSION (What did you learn?)
```

**Example:**
```
PREDICTION: "If I speak up in the meeting, I'll say something stupid and everyone will laugh"
EXPERIMENT: "I'll share one idea in tomorrow's meeting"
OUTCOME: "I shared my idea. Two people nodded. One person asked a follow-up question. No one laughed."
CONCLUSION: "My prediction was wrong. Speaking up was safer than I thought."
```

### 4. Activity Scheduling

**Purpose:** Improve mood through engaging activities

**Steps:**
1. **List activities** you used to enjoy or might enjoy
2. **Rate mastery** (how good are you at it?) and **pleasure** (how enjoyable?) for each
3. **Schedule** activities for the coming week
4. **After doing them**, re-rate mastery and pleasure
5. **Identify patterns** — what increased mood?

**Example Activities:**
- Walk in nature
- Call a friend
- Cook a favorite meal
- Listen to music
- Read something inspiring
- Create something (art, writing, craft)

### 5. Gratitude Practice

**Purpose:** Shift focus from negatives to positives

**Daily Practice:**
1. Write 3 things that went well today
2. For each, write why it happened
3. Notice how you feel after writing

**Example:**
```
1. A stranger held the door for me
   - Why: People can be kind unexpectedly

2. I finished a work task I'd been putting off
   - Why: I broke it into small steps and focused

3. My coffee tasted perfect this morning
   - Why: I took time to make it mindfully
```

## CBT-Informed Journaling Prompts

### For Anxiety

- "What evidence do you have that the worst-case scenario will happen?"
- "What's the most likely outcome, not the best or worst?"
- "What coping skills do you have if challenges arise?"
- "What would you tell a friend who's worried about this?"
- "On a scale of 0-100, how likely is this to happen? What's a more realistic rating?"

### For Depression

- "What's one small thing you could do today that might help?"
- "What evidence do you have that things will always be this way?"
- "What's one thing that went OK today, even briefly?"
- "If a friend felt this way, what would you tell them?"
- "What activity used to bring you joy, even a little?"

### For Self-Criticism

- "Would you say this to a friend? If not, why say it to yourself?"
- "What would a compassionate friend say to you right now?"
- "What's true about you that contradicts this criticism?"
- "What evidence do you have that you're 'never good enough'?"
- "What would your life look like if you treated yourself with kindness?"

### For Perfectionism

- "What does 'good enough' look like in this situation?"
- "What's the cost of holding yourself to this standard?"
- "Who benefits from you being easier on yourself?"
- "What's the most realistic outcome if you do your best but not perfectly?"
- "What would matter most to you about this situation a year from now?"

### For Procrastination

- "What emotion comes up when you think about this task?"
- "What's the smallest possible first step you could take?"
- "What would happen if you did it poorly vs. not at all?"
- "What's the evidence that you'll do it badly?"
- "If you completed this, how would you feel afterward?"

## Safety Guidelines for CBT Techniques

### What CBT Journaling IS

- Educational tool for understanding thought patterns
- Self-reflection practice for personal growth
- Skill-building for emotional awareness
- Complement to professional therapy

### What CBT Journaling IS NOT

- Not a replacement for therapy
- Not appropriate for severe depression, anxiety, or trauma
- Not suitable for active crisis situations
- Not clinical treatment

### When to Refer to Professional

**Immediately if:**
- Crisis keywords present (self-harm, suicide)
- Symptoms are severe or persistent
- Functioning is significantly impaired
- User asks for clinical help

**Consider referral if:**
- Journaling isn't helping or is making things worse
- Thoughts are becoming more distressing
- User is stuck in patterns they can't shift
- User requests professional support

## CBT Framework Language

### Phrases to Use

✅ "CBT identifies common thinking patterns..."
✅ "Research in cognitive therapy suggests..."
✅ "Many people find it helpful to examine..."
✅ "Some cognitive patterns include..."
✅ "Evidence-based CBT techniques involve..."

### Phrases to Avoid

❌ "You have cognitive distortions"
❌ "Your thinking is irrational"
❌ "You need to change your thoughts"
❌ "CBT will cure your anxiety"
❌ "This is a CBT treatment"

## Application Checklist

When applying CBT framework:

- [ ] Present techniques as educational information
- [ ] Use examples to illustrate, not diagnose
- [ ] Include appropriate disclaimer
- [ ] Suggest professional support if clinically indicated
- [ ] Stay within scope: education and self-reflection
- [ ] Don't promise outcomes
- [ ] Refer out for severe or persistent symptoms
