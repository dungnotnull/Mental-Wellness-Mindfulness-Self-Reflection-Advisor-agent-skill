# ACT Framework — Acceptance and Commitment Therapy Principles

**Purpose:** Operationalize ACT principles into concrete techniques for values work, psychological flexibility, and defusion. These are educational tools, not clinical interventions.

> **Source:** Based on Hayes, Strosahl, & Wilson (1999) Acceptance and Commitment Therapy; Hayes (2004) ACT and the Third Wave; and subsequent ACT research.

## Core ACT Model

### Psychological Flexibility = Openness + Awareness + Engagement

```
               PRESENT MOMENT AWARENESS
                      ↓
    ┌─────────────────────────────┐
    │                             │
OPEN TO EXPERIENCE ←→ VALUES-GUIDED ACTION
(Acceptance)                 (Committed Action)
    │                             │
    └─────────────────────────────┘
              ↓
           COGNITIVE DEFUSION
      (Relating differently to thoughts)
```

**Key Insight:** Struggling with thoughts and feelings often makes problems worse. Flexibility comes from opening to experience while taking action on what matters.

## The Six ACT Processes

### 1. Acceptance (Openness)

**Definition:** Making space for difficult thoughts, feelings, and sensations without trying to change, avoid, or control them.

**Key Principle:** Control is the problem, not the solution.

**Educational Language:**
- "Making room for difficult feelings"
- "Allowing thoughts to be there without struggle"
- "Dropping the rope in the tug-of-war with experience"

**Practice:**
```
1. Notice the difficult thought/feeling
2. Acknowledge it: "I'm having the thought that..." or "I notice I'm feeling..."
3. Make space: Breathe around it, allow it to be present
4. Continue: What matters to you right now?
```

### 2. Cognitive Defusion

**Definition:** Relating to thoughts as just thoughts — not facts, not commands, not threats.

**Key Principle:** You are not your thoughts. You are the one having the thoughts.

**Educational Language:**
- "Noticing thoughts as just words in your mind"
- "Watching thoughts come and go"
- "Holding thoughts lightly rather than tightly"

**Defusion Techniques:**

#### Labeling
- "I'm having the thought that..."
- "I notice I'm thinking..."
- "My mind is telling me..."

#### Silly Voice
- Say the thought in a cartoon character voice
- Sing it to a silly tune
- Notice how the feeling changes

#### Leaves on a Stream
- Place each thought on a leaf
- Watch it float down the stream
- Notice new leaves arriving

#### Observer Exercise
- Ask: "Who is noticing this thought?"
- Rest in the awareness of being the observer
- You are the context, not the content

### 3. Contact with the Present Moment

**Definition:** Being fully present, here and now, with psychological flexibility.

**Key Principle:** Life happens in the present, not in the past or future.

**Educational Language:**
- "Bringing attention to right now"
- "Noticing what's present in this moment"
- "Connecting to direct experience"

**Practice:**
```
1. Notice 5 things you can see
2. Notice 4 things you can touch
3. Notice 3 things you can hear
4. Notice 2 things you can smell
5. Notice 1 thing you can taste

Return to present-moment awareness throughout the day.
```

### 4. Self-as-Context

**Definition:** Accessing a transcendent sense of self — the you that is having thoughts, feelings, and experiences.

**Key Principle:** You are not your thoughts, feelings, or experiences. You are the space where they happen.

**Educational Language:**
- "The you that has been there through everything"
- "The observing self" vs. "the thinking self"
- "The context, not the content"

**Practice:**
```
1. Notice thoughts coming and going
2. Notice feelings coming and going
3. Ask: "Who is noticing?"
4. Notice there is a "you" that has always been there
5. Rest in that awareness
```

### 5. Values

**Definition:** Chosen life directions — what you want your life to be about.

**Key Principle:** Values are like a compass — they guide direction, not destination.

**Educational Language:**
- "What matters most to you?"
- "What kind of person do you want to be?"
- "What do you want your life to stand for?"

**Values Domains:**

| Domain | Questions |
|--------|-----------|
| **Family** | What kind of sibling/parent/child do you want to be? |
| **Friendships** | What kind of friend do you want to be? |
| **Intimate Relationships** | What kind of partner do you want to be? |
| **Work/Career** | What kind of colleague/worker do you want to be? |
| **Education/Personal Growth** | What do you want to learn? |
| **Recreation/Fun** | brings you joy and aliveness? |
| **Health/Physical Well-being** | How do you want to treat your body? |
| **Community/Environment** | What kind of citizen do you want to be? |
| **Spirituality** | What gives your life meaning? |

**Values Clarification Questions:**
- "Deep down, what matters to you in this area?"
- "If you were living your values here, what would you be doing differently?"
- "What kind of person do you want to be in this domain?"
- "What would you want people to say about you in this area at your life celebration?"

### 6. Committed Action

**Definition:** Taking effective action guided by values, with awareness and openness.

**Key Principle:** Values without action are just wishes. Action without values is just busywork.

**Educational Language:**
- "Taking steps toward what matters"
- "Living your values in small ways each day"
- "Choosing your next move based on what's important"

**SMART Goal Framework:**
```
Specific: What exactly will you do?
Measurable: How will you know you did it?
Achievable: Is it realistic?
Relevant: Does it connect to your values?
Time-bound: When will you do it?
```

**Example:**
```
Value: Being a supportive friend
Committed Action: "Call one friend this week to check in, not to ask for anything"

Specific: Call one friend
Measurable: I'll know I did it when the call is complete
Achievable: Yes, I have time for one call
Relevant: Connects to my value of being supportive
Time-bound: This week
```

## ACT Techniques for Journaling

### 1. Values Assessment

**Complete this exercise for each life domain:**

```
DOMAIN: [Family/Friendships/Work/etc.]

1. What do I value in this area?
   [List 3-5 core values]

2. What kind of person do I want to be in this domain?
   [Free-write your vision]

3. If I were fully living my values here, what would I be doing?
   [List specific actions]

4. What gets in the way of living these values?
   [Identify barriers]

5. What's one small step I could take this week?
   [Choose one achievable action]
```

### 2. Barriers to Values-Based Living

**Identify common barriers:**

| Barrier Type | Example | Defusion Strategy |
|--------------|---------|-------------------|
| **Thoughts** | "I'm not good enough" | "I'm having the thought that..." |
| **Feelings** | Anxiety, sadness | Make space, don't struggle |
| **Situations** | Lack of time, resources | Problem-solve or accept |
| **Memories** | Past failures | Notice as memory, present is new |

**Journaling Prompt:**
"What shows up when you try to move toward your values? Can you make space for it while taking action?"

### 3. The Bull's Eye Values Assessment

**For each domain, rate:**

```
1 = Not at all living my values
10 = Fully living my values

DOMAIN: Family
Current: [1-10] → Where are you now?
Goal: [1-10] → Where would you like to be?
Gap: [What's the difference?]

DOMAIN: Work
Current: [1-10]
Goal: [1-10]
Gap: [What's the difference?]

[Repeat for all domains]
```

### 4. Values-in-Action Journaling

**Daily reflection:**
```
1. What values did I live today?
   [List specific values and actions]

2. What values did I move away from today?
   [Notice without judgment]

3. What showed up when I tried to live my values?
   [Thoughts, feelings, barriers]

4. How did I respond to those barriers?
   [Notice your patterns]

5. Tomorrow, what's one way to live my values?
   [Choose one specific action]
```

## ACT-Informed Journaling Prompts

### For Values Work

- "What would you want people to say about you at your life celebration?"
- "If fear weren't a factor, what would you be doing differently?"
- "What kind of [friend/partner/parent/worker] do you want to be?"
- "What matters to you in this situation?"
- "Ten years from now, what will you wish you had done today?"
- "If you were living your values, what would be different?"

### For Defusion

- "I'm having the thought that [complete the sentence]"
- "What would this thought look like if you wrote it on a leaf and watched it float away?"
- "If a friend had this thought, what would you tell them?"
- "Can you notice this thought as just words in your mind?"
- "What's the function of this thought? Does it help or hinder?"

### For Acceptance

- "What feelings are present? Can you make space for them?"
- "If you stopped struggling with this feeling, what would happen?"
- "What would it be like to allow this feeling to be here while you do what matters?"
- "Can you breathe around this sensation?"
- "What would it mean to drop the rope in this tug-of-war?"

### For Committed Action

- "What's one small step toward your values you could take today?"
- "If you couldn't fail, what would you try?"
- "What action aligns with your values in this situation?"
- "What's the cost of inaction in this area?"
- "Who benefits when you live your values in this way?"

## Safety Guidelines for ACT Techniques

### What ACT Work IS

- Educational tool for values clarification
- Self-reflection practice for psychological flexibility
- Skill-building for present-moment awareness
- Complement to professional therapy

### What ACT Work IS NOT

- Not a replacement for therapy
- Not appropriate for severe trauma or psychosis
- Not suitable for active crisis situations
- Not clinical treatment

### When to Refer to Professional

**Immediately if:**
- Crisis keywords present (self-harm, suicide)
- Psychotic symptoms present
- Symptoms are severe or persistent
- User asks for clinical help

**Consider referral if:**
- Values work is increasing distress
- Defusion isn't providing relief
- User is stuck in patterns they can't shift
- User requests professional support

## ACT Framework Language

### Phrases to Use

✅ "ACT emphasizes making space for difficult experiences"
✅ "Values work involves clarifying what matters most"
✅ "Defusion techniques help us relate differently to thoughts"
✅ "Psychological flexibility involves openness and engagement"
✅ "Mindfulness in ACT means present-moment awareness"

### Phrases to Avoid

❌ "You need to accept your feelings"
❌ "Your thoughts are irrational"
❌ "You should commit to these values"
❌ "ACT will cure your anxiety"
❌ "This is an ACT treatment"

## Application Checklist

When applying ACT framework:

- [ ] Present techniques as educational information
- [ ] Use examples to illustrate, not diagnose
- [ ] Include appropriate disclaimer
- [ ] Suggest professional support if clinically indicated
- [ ] Stay within scope: education and self-reflection
- [ ] Don't promise outcomes
- [ ] Refer out for severe or persistent symptoms
