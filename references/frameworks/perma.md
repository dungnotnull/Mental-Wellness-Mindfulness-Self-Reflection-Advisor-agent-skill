# PERMA Framework — Positive Psychology Wellbeing Model

**Purpose:** Operationalize PERMA principles into concrete wellbeing practices and education. These are educational tools, not clinical interventions.

> **Source:** Based on Seligman (2011) Flourish; positive psychology research on wellbeing, meaning, and fulfillment.

## What Is PERMA?

**Definition:** PERMA is an evidence-based model of wellbeing developed by Martin Seligman, identifying five pillars of flourishing: Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, and Accomplishment.

**Key Insight:** Wellbeing is multidimensional — it's not just about feeling good, but about functioning well across multiple domains of life.

**Educational Language:**
- "PERMA offers a framework for understanding wellbeing"
- "Research suggests five key areas contribute to flourishing"
- "Positive psychology has identified pathways to wellbeing"
- "Wellbeing involves multiple dimensions, not just happiness"

## The PERMA Model

### P - Positive Emotion

**Definition:** Experiencing positive feelings about the past, present, and future.

**Key Components:**
- **Past:** Gratitude, satisfaction, contentment
- **Present:** Mindfulness, flow, pleasure, joy
- **Future:** Optimism, hope, faith

**Educational Language:**
- "Positive emotion involves experiencing pleasant feelings"
- "Gratitude practices can enhance positive emotion"
- "Research suggests savoring positive experiences builds wellbeing"

**Evidence-Based Practices:**
```
GRATITUDE:
- Three Good Things: Write 3 things that went well daily
- Gratitude Visit: Write and deliver a thank-you letter
- Gratitude Journal: Note things you're grateful for

SAVORING:
- Share positive experiences with others
- Memory building: Take photos, keep mementos
- Sharpening senses: Notice sensory details of positive experiences
- Self-congratulation: Acknowledge your own contributions

OPTIMISM:
- Best Possible Self: Write about your future best self
- Growth Mindset: View challenges as opportunities
- Solution-Focused Thinking: Ask "how" instead of "why" problems
```

### E - Engagement

**Definition:** Being fully absorbed and interested in activities, leading to flow states.

**Key Components:**
- **Flow:** Complete absorption in an activity
- **Strengths Use:** Applying your signature strengths
- **Loss of Self-Consciousness:** Time disappears, self fades

**Educational Language:**
- "Engagement involves being fully absorbed in activities"
- "Flow states occur when challenge matches skill"
- "Using strengths increases engagement and wellbeing"

**Evidence-Based Practices:**
```
FLOW:
- Identify activities where you lose track of time
- Balance challenge and skill level
- Eliminate distractions during flow activities
- Notice and reflect on flow experiences

STRENGTHS USE:
- Identify your signature strengths
- Find new ways to use existing strengths
- Develop strengths through deliberate practice
- Apply strengths in different life domains

ENGAGEMENT AUDIT:
- When are you most engaged?
- What activities absorb your attention?
- What strengths are you using when engaged?
- How could you increase engagement?
```

### R - Relationships

**Definition:** Feeling connected to, supported by, and significant to others.

**Key Components:**
- **Social Connection:** Feeling understood and valued
- **Intimacy:** Deep, authentic sharing
- **Support:** Having people to rely on
- **Contribution:** Supporting others

**Educational Language:**
- "Relationships are fundamental to wellbeing"
- "Social connection supports mental and physical health"
- "Quality relationships are built on mutual support"

**Evidence-Based Practices:**
```
CONNECTION PRACTICES:
- Active Constructive Responding: Respond enthusiastically to good news
- Shared Meaningful Activities: Do things together that matter
- Express Appreciation: Regular gratitude toward others
- Regular Check-Ins: Meaningful conversations beyond logistics

RELATIONSHIP MAINTENANCE:
- Schedule regular time with important people
- Share vulnerable experiences appropriately
- Practice forgiveness and repair
- Celebrate others' successes

COMMUNITY BUILDING:
- Join groups around shared interests
- Volunteer for causes you care about
- Participate in community events
- Reach out to new connections
```

### M - Meaning

**Definition:** Feeling part of and serving something bigger than the self.

**Key Components:**
- **Purpose:** Having aims and aspirations
- **Values:** Knowing what matters
- **Contribution:** Making a difference
- **Transcendence:** Connection to something larger

**Educational Language:**
- "Meaning involves connecting to something beyond yourself"
- "Purpose and values guide meaningful action"
- "Contribution and service enhance wellbeing"

**Evidence-Based Practices:**
```
MEANING CLARIFICATION:
- Values Clarification: Identify what truly matters
- Purpose Statement: Write your life purpose
- Legacy Reflection: Consider what you want to be remembered for
- Meaning Audit: Assess current alignment with meaning

CONTRIBUTION:
- Identify how you already contribute
- Find new ways to make a difference
- Acts of kindness (large and small)
- Mentorship and teaching

TRANSCENDENCE:
- Connect to nature, art, music, spirituality
- Consider your role in larger systems
- Participate in collective action
- Engage with philosophical/spiritual questions
```

### A - Accomplishment

**Definition:** Pursuing and achieving goals that provide a sense of mastery and competence.

**Key Components:**
- **Goal Pursuit:** Working toward aims
- **Mastery:** Developing skills
- **Achievement:** Accomplishing tasks
- **Growth:** Learning and improving

**Educational Language:**
- "Accomplishment involves pursuing and achieving goals"
- "Mastery and growth contribute to wellbeing"
- "Progress toward goals matters, not just completion"

**Evidence-Based Practices:**
```
GOAL SETTING:
- SMART Goals: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound
- Values-Aligned Goals: Connect goals to what matters
- Process Goals: Focus on actions, not just outcomes
- Growth Goals: Include learning and development

MASTERY DEVELOPMENT:
- Deliberate Practice: Focused skill-building
- Feedback Integration: Learn from performance
- Progress Tracking: Notice improvement over time
- Challenge Stretching: Gradually increase difficulty

ACHIEVEMENT CELEBRATION:
- Acknowledge small wins
- Reflect on progress
- Share achievements with supporters
- Practice self-recognition
```

## Domain-Specific Practices

### Positive Emotion Practices

```
THREE GOOD THINGS (Daily):
1. Write three things that went well today
2. For each, write why it happened
3. Notice how this reflection affects your mood

SAVORING WALK (Weekly):
Take a walk and intentionally savor:
- Notice beauty in nature
- Feel gratitude for your body's movement
- Appreciate the freedom to walk
- Share the experience with someone later

OPTIMISM REFLECTION (Weekly):
1. What are you looking forward to this week?
2. What opportunities might appear?
3. What strengths will you use?
4. What's your best possible outcome?
```

### Engagement Practices

```
STRENGTHS EXPLORATION:
1. Take a strengths assessment (VIA Character Strengths)
2. Identify your top 5 strengths
3. For each strength, find one new way to use it this week

FLOW ACTIVITY INVENTORY:
1. List activities where you lose track of time
2. For each, identify what makes it engaging
3. Schedule regular time for these activities
4. Protect this time from distractions

ENGAGEMENT BOOST:
Pick one routine activity this week:
- Approach it with curiosity
- Notice new details
- Find a way to use a strength
- Reflect on what engaged you
```

### Relationship Practices

```
ACTIVE CONSTRUCTIVE RESPONDING:
When someone shares good news:
- Listen enthusiastically
- Ask for details
- Relive the experience with them
- Celebrate their success

GRADITUDE LETTER:
1. Think of someone who helped you
2. Write a detailed thank-you letter
3. Deliver it in person if possible
4. Read it aloud to them

CONNECTION CHECK-IN:
Schedule regular conversations:
- Ask: "How are you really?"
- Listen without solving
- Share vulnerably too
- Express appreciation
```

### Meaning Practices

```
VALUES CLARIFICATION:
For each life domain (work, relationships, growth, etc.):
1. What matters to me here?
2. What kind of person do I want to be?
3. What does living my values look like?

CONTRIBUTION BRAINSTORM:
1. What problems do you care about?
2. What skills do you have?
3. Where do these intersect?
4. What small contribution could you make?

LEGACY REFLECTION:
1. What do you want to be remembered for?
2. What impact do you want to have?
3. Who do you want to help?
4. What mark do you want to leave?
```

### Accomplishment Practices

```
SMART GOAL WORKSHEET:
Specific: What exactly will you do?
Measurable: How will you know it's done?
Achievable: Is it realistic?
Relevant: Does it matter to you?
Time-bound: When will you do it?

PROGRESS TRACKING:
1. What goal are you working toward?
2. What's one step you can take this week?
3. What's one small win you've had?
4. What have you learned so far?

MASTERY PLAN:
1. What skill do you want to develop?
2. What's your current level?
3. What deliberate practice will you do?
4. How will you know you're improving?
```

## PERMA Language Guidelines

### Phrases to Use

✅ "Research suggests positive emotion contributes to wellbeing"
✅ "PERMA identifies five pillars of flourishing"
✅ "Evidence shows relationships support mental health"
✅ "Meaning and purpose enhance life satisfaction"
✅ "Goal pursuit and accomplishment contribute to wellbeing"

### Phrases to Avoid

❌ "You need to improve your PERMA score"
❌ "Your wellbeing is low because you lack meaning"
❌ "Positive psychology will fix depression"
❌ "You should have better relationships"
❌ "You're not flourishing enough"

## Common PERMA Misunderstandings

### Misunderstanding: "PERMA Is About Being Happy"

**Clarification:**
"PERMA is about overall wellbeing, not just happiness. It includes positive emotion AND engagement, relationships, meaning, and accomplishment. Some aspects of wellbeing involve effort and challenge, not just pleasure."

### Misunderstanding: "You Need High Scores in All Domains"

**Clarification:**
"PERMA domains fluctuate. Low scores in some domains are normal. What matters is overall balance and intentional focus on areas that need attention. Perfection isn't the goal."

### Misunderstanding: "PERMA Replaces Professional Help"

**Clarification:**
"PERMA is an educational framework for understanding wellbeing. It's not treatment for mental health conditions. For clinical concerns, professional support is appropriate."

## Safety Notes

**Wellbeing work can bring up:**
- Awareness of life areas needing attention
- Discomfort about change
- Comparison and self-judgment
- Grief about misalignment

**If overwhelmed:**
- Focus on one domain at a time
- Remember: growth is gradual
- Be kind about where you are
- Consider professional support if needed

**Consider professional support if:**
- Wellbeing feels persistently low
- Multiple domains feel chronically misaligned
- You feel stuck despite efforts
- Symptoms of depression/anxiety appear

These practices are educational tools.
For deeper wellbeing work, consider positive psychology therapy or coaching with a licensed professional.
