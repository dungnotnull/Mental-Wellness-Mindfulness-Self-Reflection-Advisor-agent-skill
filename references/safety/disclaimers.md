# Disclaimers — Required Language Templates

**Purpose:** Define standard disclaimer templates for all responses. These ensure the skill stays within appropriate scope and encourages professional consultation.

## Disclaimer Hierarchy

### 1. Full Standard Disclaimer (Most Responses)

**When to use:** All responses except crisis-only responses

```markdown
**Disclaimer:** This response provides general, educational/analytical information only.
It is not a substitute for advice from a qualified mental health professional.
Always consult with a licensed therapist, counselor, or psychiatrist before making
decisions based on this information.

If you are experiencing a mental health emergency or crisis, please contact emergency
services or a crisis hotline immediately.
```

### 2. Crisis-Enhanced Disclaimer (Crisis-Related Content)

**When to use:** Responses that include crisis resources or address crisis situations

```markdown
**Disclaimer:** This response provides general educational information and crisis resources.
It is not a substitute for emergency services or professional mental health care.

**If you are in immediate danger, please call emergency services (911 in the US)
or go to your nearest emergency room.**

**Crisis Resources:**
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** Dial or text 988
- **Crisis Text Line:** Text HOME to 741741
- **International:** https://findahelpline.com/

This service is not equipped to handle crisis situations. For immediate support,
please use the resources listed above.
```

### 3. Educational Content Disclaimer (Framework Explanations)

**When to use:** When explaining psychological frameworks, research, or theory

```markdown
**Educational Disclaimer:** The following information is presented for educational
purposes to support understanding and self-reflection. It is not clinical advice,
diagnosis, or treatment. For personalized guidance, consult with a qualified
mental health professional.

The frameworks discussed (CBT, ACT, MBSR, PERMA) are evidence-based approaches
commonly used in mental health care. Application of these concepts is best done
with guidance from a licensed practitioner.
```

### 4. Assessment Disclaimer (Self-Reflection Tools)

**When to use:** When providing reflection prompts, checklists, or self-assessment tools

```markdown
**Assessment Disclaimer:** The following prompts and reflections are for
self-exploration purposes only. They are not diagnostic tools and do not
constitute a mental health assessment.

Scores or reflections from these exercises are not clinical determinations.
For proper evaluation, please consult with a licensed mental health professional
who can provide comprehensive assessment tailored to your needs.
```

### 5. Framework-Specific Disclaimers

#### CBT-Focused Responses

```markdown
**CBT Disclaimer:** Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is a well-established
therapeutic approach. The information provided here reflects CBT principles for
educational purposes. Learning CBT techniques is most effective with guidance
from a trained CBT therapist.

For help finding a CBT practitioner, consider:
- Association for Behavioral and Cognitive Therapies (ABCT): https://abct.org/
- Psychology Today therapist directory: https://www.psychologytoday.com/
```

#### ACT-Focused Responses

```markdown
**ACT Disclaimer:** Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) is a mindfulness-based
approach to psychological flexibility. The concepts shared here are for educational
purposes. ACT is most effective when practiced with guidance from a trained ACT
therapist.

For help finding an ACT practitioner, consider:
- Association for Contextual Behavioral Science (ACBS): https://contextualscience.org/
- Psychology Today therapist directory: https://www.psychologytoday.com/
```

#### MBSR-Focused Responses

```markdown
**MBSR Disclaimer:** Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) is an evidence-based
program for stress and pain management. The exercises shared here are simplified
versions for educational purposes. For full MBSR training, consider an 8-week
MBSR course led by a certified instructor.

For more information on MBSR:
- Center for Mindfulness (UMass): https://www.umassmed.edu/cfm/
- MBSR teacher directory: https://www.mbsr.link/directory
```

## Disclaimer Placement

### Standard Response Structure

All responses follow this structure:

```markdown
[Direct Response to User]
[Framework Information if applicable]
[Prompts or Exercises if applicable]
---
[Disclaimer]
---
[Additional Resources if applicable]
```

### Example Response

```markdown
Here are some reflective journaling prompts to help you process this situation:

**1. Situation Description**
What happened? Describe the situation objectively.

**2. Emotion Check**
What emotions came up? Rate their intensity (1-10).

**3. Thought Exploration**
What thoughts went through your mind?

**4. Alternative Perspectives**
What other viewpoints might exist?

**5. Values Alignment**
What matters to you in how you respond to this?

---

**Disclaimer:** This response provides general, educational/analytical information only.
It is not a substitute for advice from a qualified mental health professional.
Always consult with a licensed therapist, counselor, or psychiatrist before making
decisions based on this information.

If you are experiencing a mental health emergency or crisis, please contact emergency
services or a crisis hotline immediately.

---

**For ongoing support, consider:**
- Speaking with a licensed therapist
- Journaling regularly to track patterns
- discussing these reflections with a trusted support person
```

## Crisis Response Template

When crisis keywords are detected, use this complete response:

```markdown
**I hear that you're going through a very difficult time.**

Your wellbeing matters, and there are people who want to help.

---

**Immediate Crisis Resources**

**If you are in immediate danger, please call emergency services (911 in the US)
or go to your nearest emergency room.**

**United States:**
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** Dial or text 988 (available 24/7)
- **Crisis Text Line:** Text HOME to 741741
- **Veterans Crisis Line:** Dial 988 then press 1, or text 838255

**International:**
- **Find A Helpline:** https://findahelpline.com/ (crisis support worldwide)
- **Befrienders Worldwide:** https://www.befrienders.org/

---

**You are not alone.** Help is available 24/7, and people care about you.
Please reach out to one of these resources or contact a trusted adult,
friend, or family member.

**This service is not equipped to handle crisis situations.** For immediate
support, please use the resources listed above or go to your nearest
emergency room.

---

**Disclaimer:** This response provides crisis resources for educational and
informational purposes. It is not a substitute for emergency services or
professional mental health care.
```

## Professional Referral Template

When professional consultation is appropriate but crisis is not present:

```markdown
**Considering Professional Support**

Based on what you've shared, speaking with a mental health professional could
be valuable. A licensed therapist can provide:

- Personalized assessment and guidance
- Evidence-based strategies tailored to your needs
- Support in developing coping skills
- A safe space to explore your concerns

**How to Find a Therapist:**
- **Psychology Today:** https://www.psychologytoday.com/ (search by location, insurance, specialty)
- **Your insurance:** Call the number on your insurance card for in-network providers
- **Employee Assistance Program (EAP):** If your workplace offers one
- **Primary Care Doctor:** Can provide referrals to mental health providers

**Types of Providers:**
- **Licensed Professional Counselor (LPC):** Master's-level counseling
- **Clinical Social Worker (LCSW):** Master's-level social work
- **Psychologist (PhD/PsyD):** Doctoral-level psychology, testing, therapy
- **Psychiatrist (MD):** Medical doctor, can prescribe medication
- **Psychiatric Nurse Practitioner (PMHNP):** Can prescribe medication

**What to Consider:**
- Do you prefer someone with a particular framework (CBT, ACT, etc.)?
- Do you need someone who works with specific concerns (anxiety, trauma, etc.)?
- What can you afford? (Sliding scale, insurance, private pay)
- When are you available? (Evenings, weekends, daytime?)

---

**Disclaimer:** This response provides general information only. It is not a
substitute for advice from a qualified mental health professional.
```

## Framework Explanation Disclaimers

When explaining psychological frameworks:

```markdown
**About CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy)**

CBT is a well-researched approach that focuses on the relationship between
thoughts, feelings, and behaviors. It's based on the idea that our thoughts
influence how we feel and act.

**Key CBT Concepts:**

1. **Cognitive Restructuring:** Identifying and examining thought patterns
2. **Behavioral Activation:** Taking actions that improve mood
3. **Exposure:** Gradually approaching feared situations
4. **Skill Building:** Developing coping and problem-solving skills

**Note:** Learning CBT techniques is most effective with guidance from a
trained CBT therapist. The information shared here is for educational purposes.

---

**Educational Disclaimer:** The information presented is for educational
purposes to support understanding. It is not clinical advice, diagnosis, or
treatment. For personalized guidance, consult with a qualified mental health
professional.
```

## Minimal Disclaimer (Brief Interactions)

For very brief responses where standard disclaimer would be disproportionate:

```markdown
*Note: This is general information for educational purposes. For personalized
guidance, consult with a qualified mental health professional.*
```

## Disclaimer Compliance Checklist

Before finalizing any response, verify:

- [ ] Disclaimer is appropriate to response type
- [ ] Disclaimer is placed before additional resources
- [ ] Disclaimer is formatted clearly (Markdown bold for key text)
- [ ] Crisis resources included if any concern is present
- [ ] Professional referral suggested if clinically indicated
- [ ] No disclaimer is "watered down" or minimized
- [ ] Language remains clear and accessible

## Accessibility Considerations

Ensure disclaimers are:
- Written in plain language (avoid jargon)
- Formatted with clear Markdown structure
- Placed consistently (always before resources)
- Clearly visible (bold for emphasis)
- Translated if language preferences are known
