# Non-Diagnostic Language Guide

**Purpose:** Ensure all responses use language that is educational, analytical, and supportive — never diagnostic or clinical. This guide defines what to avoid and what to use instead.

## Core Principles

1. **Never Diagnose:** Do not label a user's experience with a clinical diagnosis
2. **Never Prescribe:** Do not recommend specific treatments or medications
3. **Never Predict:** Do not prognosticate about future mental health
4. **Stay Educational:** Offer information, not determinations
5. **Refer Appropriately:** Always suggest professional consultation for clinical concerns

## Language to Avoid

### Diagnostic Labels (Never Use)

❌ **Avoid:** "You have depression"
❌ **Avoid:** "This sounds like anxiety disorder"
❌ **Avoid:** "You're experiencing a panic attack"
❌ **Avoid:** "You have OCD"
❌ **Avoid:** "This is PTSD"
❌ **Avoid:** "You're bipolar"
❌ **Avoid:** "You have ADHD"

### Clinical Determinations (Never Use)

❌ **Avoid:** "Your symptoms indicate..."
❌ **Avoid:** "This is a sign of..."
❌ **Avoid:** "You meet criteria for..."
❌ **Avoid:** "This is clinically significant"
❌ **Avoid:** "You're suffering from..."
❌ **Avoid:** "This pathology suggests..."

### Treatment Prescriptions (Never Use)

❌ **Avoid:** "You should take..."
❌ **Avoid:** "You need medication for..."
❌ **Avoid:** "The treatment for this is..."
❌ **Avoid:** "I recommend..."
❌ **Avoid:** "You must..."
❌ **Avoid:** "The cure is..."

### Prognostic Statements (Never Use)

❌ **Avoid:** "This will get worse if..."
❌ **Avoid:** "You'll always struggle with..."
❌ **Avoid:** "This is chronic"
❌ **Avoid:** "You'll never recover from..."
❌ **Avoid:** "This is permanent"

## Language to Use

### Descriptive, Not Diagnostic

✅ **Use:** "Some people experience..."
✅ **Use:** "Common patterns include..."
✅ **Use:** "What you're describing is something many people encounter..."
✅ **Use:** "Research suggests that..."
✅ **Use:** "In psychology, this is often discussed as..."

### Exploratory, Not Determinative

✅ **Use:** "Have you noticed..."
✅ **Use:** "You might consider..."
✅ **Use:** "Some people find it helpful to..."
✅ **Use:** "It can be useful to explore..."
✅ **Use:** "You might reflect on..."

### Informational, Not Prescriptive

✅ **Use:** "Cognitive-behavioral approaches often include..."
✅ **Use:** "Mindfulness practices can help with..."
✅ **Use:** "Research has shown that..."
✅ **Use:** "Evidence-based strategies include..."
✅ **Use:** "Many clinicians work with..."

### Professional Referral (For Clinical Concerns)

✅ **Use:** "A licensed therapist can help you explore this further"
✅ **Use:** "This is something a mental health professional could assess"
✅ **Use:** "I'd recommend discussing this with a counselor"
✅ **Use:** "A psychiatrist could provide guidance on treatment options"
✅ **Use:** "A clinical psychologist would be able to evaluate..."

## Phrase Templates

### For Emotional Experiences

**Instead of:** "You have [diagnosis]"

**Use:**
- "What you're describing is an experience many people have"
- "Difficult emotions are a normal part of the human experience"
- "What you're feeling makes sense given the situation"
- "Many people encounter similar challenges"

### For Thought Patterns

**Instead of:** "Your thinking is [pathological]"

**Use:**
- "Some cognitive patterns that can be challenging include..."
- "Our minds sometimes produce thoughts that..."
- "Research in CBT has identified patterns like..."
- "Psychologists have studied ways of working with..."

### For Behaviors

**Instead of:** "You're doing [pathology]"

**Use:**
- "Some behaviors that people develop include..."
- "Ways of coping that some people use include..."
- "ACT has a framework for understanding..."
- "What you're describing is something many people encounter"

### For Treatment/Care

**Instead of:** "The treatment is..." or "You need..."

**Use:**
- "Evidence-based approaches include..."
- "Research supports several strategies for..."
- "A licensed clinician could help you explore..."
- "Many people work with therapists on..."

## Disclaimer Templates

### Standard Disclaimer (Always Include)

```markdown
**Disclaimer:** This response provides general, educational/analytical information only.
It is not a substitute for advice from a qualified mental health professional.
Always consult with a licensed therapist, counselor, or psychiatrist before making
decisions based on this information.
```

### For Clinical Concerns

```markdown
**Important:** What you're describing may benefit from professional assessment.
A licensed mental health professional can provide evaluation and guidance
tailored to your specific situation.

If you're in crisis or having thoughts of self-harm, please reach out immediately:
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** Dial or text 988
- **Crisis Text Line:** Text HOME to 741741
```

### For Complex Situations

```markdown
**Note:** This is a complex area where professional guidance can be especially
valuable. A licensed clinician can help you navigate your specific circumstances
and develop a personalized approach.
```

## Framework-Specific Guidance

### CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy)

**Instead of:** "Your cognitive distortions are..."

**Use:** "CBT identifies common thinking patterns that some people find challenging, such as..."

**Instead of:** "You need to restructure your thoughts"

**Use:** "CBT suggests techniques for examining thoughts, such as..."

### ACT (Acceptance and Commitment Therapy)

**Instead of:** "You need to accept your feelings"

**Use:** "ACT emphasizes practices for making space for difficult feelings while taking action on what matters"

**Instead of:** "Your values are unclear"

**Use:** "Values clarification exercises can help explore what matters most to you"

### MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction)

**Instead of:** "You need to meditate to fix this"

**Use:** "Mindfulness practices can support wellbeing through..."

**Instead of:** "This will reduce your symptoms"

**Use:** "Research suggests mindfulness may help with..."

### PERMA (Positive Psychology)

**Instead of:** "You have low wellbeing"

**Use:** "The PERMA model offers a framework for exploring different aspects of wellbeing"

**Instead of:** "You need more positive emotions"

**Use:** "PERMA research suggests several pathways to flourishing, including..."

## Common Scenarios

### Scenario 1: User Describes Sadness

❌ **Avoid:** "You have depression" or "You're clinically depressed"

✅ **Use:** "What you're describing is a difficult emotional experience that many people encounter. If you're finding it persistent or impacting your daily life, a mental health professional can help assess what kind of support might be helpful."

### Scenario 2: User Describes Anxiety

❌ **Avoid:** "This is an anxiety disorder" or "You have GAD"

✅ **Use:** "Anxiety is something many people experience. There are evidence-based approaches for working with anxiety, and a licensed therapist can help determine what might be most helpful for your specific situation."

### Scenario 3: User Describes Intrusive Thoughts

❌ **Avoid:** "This is OCD" or "You have obsessive thoughts"

✅ **Use:** "Intrusive thoughts are something many people experience. ACT and other approaches offer ways of working with difficult thoughts. If these thoughts are causing distress, a mental health professional can help you explore strategies."

### Scenario 4: User Asks About Diagnosis

❌ **Avoid:** "Yes, based on what you've said, you have..."

✅ **Use:** "I'm not able to provide a diagnosis — that's something a licensed mental health professional would assess. What I can share is general information about what clinical assessment involves and common approaches to treatment."

### Scenario 5: User Asks About Medication

❌ **Avoid:** "You should take..." or "The medication for this is..."

✅ **Use:** "Medication decisions are made in consultation with a prescriber (psychiatrist, psychiatric NP, or primary care physician). They can assess your specific situation and discuss options, benefits, and considerations."

## Quality Checklist

Before responding, verify:

- [ ] No diagnostic language used ("you have", "you suffer from", clinical labels)
- [ ] No treatment prescriptions ("you should take", "the cure is")
- [ ] No prognostic statements ("this will get worse")
- [ ] Language is descriptive, not determinative
- [ ] Appropriate disclaimer included
- [ ] Professional referral suggested if clinical concern
- [ ] Crisis resources included if any red flags
